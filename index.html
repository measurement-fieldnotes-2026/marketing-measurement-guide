The short version

Three tools, three questions

Attribution

Which touchpoints appeared on observed journeys? Useful for diagnosing paths; not a causal estimate.

Incrementality

What happened because marketing ran versus a credible counterfactual? Design holdouts before treating lift as real.

Marketing mix model

How do broad spend patterns, time and external factors relate to outcomes? Calibrate model assumptions with experiments.

The common trap

A high reported ROAS can coexist with low incremental value when buyers would have converted anyway. Different tools answer different questions. Don't add their numbers together.

A decision-ready brief

Before shifting budget, write the decision, the outcome window, the unit of randomization or comparison, the expected lag, guardrails, and what result would reverse your call. Then write the uncertainty beside the point estimate.

The 7-point checklist

  1. 01DecisionWhat budget or product action changes if the estimate is positive, null or negative?
  2. 02PopulationWho was eligible? Keep an intent-to-treat denominator rather than only people who engaged.
  3. 03CounterfactualRandomized holdout if feasible; if using geos, check match quality, spillovers and pre-period trends.
  4. 04Outcome and lagChoose a business outcome and window before seeing the result. Avoid counting the same conversion twice.
  5. 05Power and uncertaintySize the test against a useful minimum effect. Report an interval, not only a p-value.
  6. 06GuardrailsCheck retention, margin, user experience, community health and other harms relevant to the decision.
  7. 07ReconciliationCompare experiment lift with MMM and attribution. Diagnose disagreement rather than averaging the estimates.
A quick worked example

Suppose 10,000 eligible people are assigned equally. 500 of 5,000 exposed-eligible people convert, versus 450 of 5,000 controls. The raw difference is 1 percentage point (10% versus 9%), or about 50 additional conversions among the 5,000 assigned to treatment. That alone is not a budget decision: check interval width, leakage, cost, quality, and whether the test was designed and powered before launch. These figures are illustrative, not a real study.

Use the right tool

  • For a single eligible campaign or onboarding change: randomized experiment, with unit and spillover carefully defined.
  • For markets and broad channels that cannot be randomized at person level: a planned geo test with matched pre-periods and contamination checks.
  • For portfolio planning across channels and long lags: MMM, then calibrate and stress-test it against experimental evidence.
